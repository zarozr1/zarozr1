# Rule 1: The hook (first 1.5 seconds)

Instagram and TikTok push a video to more people when viewers watch it to
the end. Most viewers decide in the first second. The hook gets more effort
than the rest of the video.

## Must

- **The first spoken word starts before 0.3 s.** No breath, no logo, no title card.
- **Motion on frame 0.** Something is already moving on the very first frame.
- **The first sentence is a contradiction, a surprising result or a direct
  "you" observation**, under 22 words. Patterns that work:
  - *Paradox:* "Your phone screen reacts to the lightest brush of your finger, and ignores a pen pressed as hard as you can."
  - *Hidden cost:* "Every time you do X, Y happens and you never notice."
  - *Big number + small action:* "This 2-gram chip moves 4 billion dollars a day."
  - *You're doing it wrong:* "You've been charging your phone wrong for years."
- **The first visual shows the hook literally.** If the line says finger and
  pen, the screen shows a finger and a pen.
- **Open a loop the video closes.** The hook raises a question the ending
  answers. Make the answer arrive in the last 20%, not the middle.

## Never

- "Hey guys", "In this video", "Did you know", "Today we're going to…"
- Starting with the answer.
- More than one idea in the hook. If the user says "the hook talks too
  much", cut it to one clause plus one twist.

## Ending

- The answer lands, then one short call to action ("Follow for the next
  one."). Under 2 seconds. No outro card longer than 1 second.
- Optionally loop: the last line flows back into the first visual so replays
  feel seamless. Replays count as watch time.
